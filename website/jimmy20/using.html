Using Jimmy Next

Jimmy Next processes QSOs in one of two modes, and builds an accessible "Stations calling" list you work through from the keyboard.

Two operating modes

Switch between them any time:

  • Call CQ mode (Alt+C) — Jimmy Next calls CQ for you, using whichever CQ variant you've selected (CQ only, CQ DX only, or both).
  • Listen for calls mode (Alt+L) — Jimmy Next listens without transmitting CQ, and still builds the calling list from everything it hears.

In either mode, stations you haven't worked yet are added to the Stations calling list. Stations calling you directly always get top priority and are moved to the top of the list.

The calling queue

You never have to manually track who's calling — Jimmy Next filters, tags, and orders incoming stations for you:

  • A station replying to your callsign always ranks above everything else.
  • Below that, categories like New DXCC, Wanted Calls, Directed CQ, and stations still needed for an active award are ranked in tiers you can fully reorder and re-weight yourself (Alt+S, the Sort Order Editor).
  • Within ordinary CQ traffic, stations are sorted by whichever method you choose — order received, most recent first, strongest/weakest signal, nearest/farthest, or by compass direction — with several methods chainable as tie-breakers.
  • Filters let you decide which categories are even admitted to the queue, and a block list keeps specific callsigns out entirely.

Focus the list with Ctrl+W, and press Alt+N to skip straight to the next available station without waiting out a timeout — one of the most useful shortcuts in Jimmy Next.

Wanted Calls

Type specific callsigns into the Wanted Calls list (Options) to always push them toward the top of the queue. There's also a separate "alert when heard anywhere" option, which plays a distinct sound the instant a wanted callsign is heard in any decode — even one you wouldn't otherwise be shown.

Jimmy Next also tags calls as wanted automatically based on your logbook: new DXCC entities, states you still need, CQ zones you still need, and stations still needed for any award you're actively tracking (see Awards & Logging).

Replying and managing a contact

  • Select a station from the calling list and Jimmy Next handles the rest of the exchange — signal reports, RR73/73 — automatically.
  • Manual Call (Alt+J) lets you type any callsign to call directly, bypassing the queue. If lookups are enabled, Jimmy Next shows a quick confirmation with the station's grid square before actually calling.
  • The Repeat limit (Transmit tab) sets how many times Jimmy Next repeats the same message to a station that isn't answering before it gives up and moves to the next call. Raise it for weak or marginal conditions; lower it to work a busy queue faster. (The old "Extended Timeout" / "Hold" toggle is gone — the Repeat limit is now the single control for this.)
  • Escape halts transmit, cancels the current QSO, and returns you to Listen mode.

Transmit frequency

A sighted WSJT-X or Nexus operator sets where in the passband they transmit and receive by dragging markers on a waterfall. Jimmy Next gives you the same control through the keyboard, with spoken confirmation and no waterfall required.

The Transmit frequency setting (Options > Transmit) chooses how Jimmy Next places your transmit audio when it starts a CQ or answers a station:

  • Best free frequency (automatic) — the default. Jimmy Next studies recent decodes and transmits in the widest clear gap it can find, so you don't land on top of anyone. Leave this on and you never have to think about frequency.
  • Hold Tx frequency — Jimmy Next keeps your current transmit frequency and only moves your receive onto the station you're working. This is the equivalent of WSJT-X's "Hold Tx Freq" being on.
  • Transmit on station's frequency — when you answer a station, both your transmit and receive move to that station's frequency. This is the classic WSJT-X double-click-to-work behavior.

In every mode, once a contact is under way Jimmy Next leaves your transmit frequency alone until the contact ends — it never re-picks a "better" spot in the middle of a QSO. When someone answers your CQ, Jimmy Next holds the frequency you were calling on and simply follows the caller on receive, the way a running operator expects.

You can also take over by hand at any time. Alt+W sets an exact transmit frequency; Shift+F12 / Shift+F11 nudge the transmit frequency up / down one step, and Ctrl+Shift+F12 / Ctrl+Shift+F11 do the same for receive (the step size is on the Transmit tab, "Frequency step", default 60 Hz); Ctrl+F12 and Ctrl+F11 copy one marker to the other (Tx ← Rx and Rx ← Tx); and Alt+V reads back both frequencies and the current mode. Using one of the transmit keys during a contact pins that contact to your manual frequency — automatic selection resumes after you Halt, finish the QSO, change band or mode, or reselect "Best free frequency." These keys pair with F11/F12 already being audio level down/up, and deliberately use no arrow keys so they don't clash with screen-reader navigation.

Split Operation (None / Rig / Fake It, on the Radio tab) is unchanged and separate: it's only how the offset is applied to your radio, not where in the passband you operate.

DX Spot Watch

Press Ctrl+4 to open a small, personal watch list of specific callsigns. For each one, Jimmy Next shows when and where it was last heard on the air in near real time — even stations you haven't decoded yourself — pulled from PSK Reporter's live spot feed. It's ideal for tracking a specific chase target: a DXpedition, a special-event station, or a POTA/SOTA activator you're waiting for. Spot Watch is intentionally separate from Wanted Calls — adding a callsign here doesn't change queue priority, it's purely "is this station even active right now, and where."
